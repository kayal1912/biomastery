:core[Define catalyst:]

- :core[a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction]

- :core[and is not changed by the reaction ]

:core[Define enzyme:]

- :core[proteins that are involved in all metabolic reactions ]
- :core[where they function as biological catalysts]

:core[Why are enzymes important: ]

- :core[they’re]:core[ important because they act as biological catalysts, speeding up chemical reactions so that metabolic processes occur fast enough to sustain life]
- :core[Without enzymes, reactions such as digestion would be too slow to keep organisms alive]

:core[Describe enzyme action:]

- :core[Enzymes have an active site that is complementary in shape to its substrate]
- :core[The substrate binds to the active site of the enzyme ]:supplement[forming an enzyme–substrate complex]
- :core[The enzyme catalyses the reaction, converting the substrate into product(s)]
- :core[The product(s) leave the active site and the enzyme remains unchanged and can be reused ]

:core[What is the effect of temperature on enzyme activity?]

- :core[as temperature increases enzyme activity increases and then decreases]
- :core[at optimum temperature, there is the highest rate of enzyme activity]
- :core[beyond optimum temperature, the enzyme is denatured (enzyme activity decreases)]

:core[What is the effect of pH on enzyme activity?]

- :core[each enzyme has an optimum pH at which it works best (e.g. pepsin works best at pH 2)]
- :core[at extreme pH values beyond that of optimum pH, the enzyme becomes denatured]

:supplement[Explain the specificity of enzymes:]

- :supplement[The active site of an enzyme (like maltase) has a specific shape that is complementary to only one particular substrate (like matlose)]
- :supplement[This means only that substrate can bind to the active site to form an enzyme–substrate complex]

:supplement[Explain the effect of changes in temperature on enzyme activity: ]

- :supplement[As temperature increases, the kinetic energy of molecules increases ]

- :supplement[The active site changes shape and is no longer complementary to the substrate]
