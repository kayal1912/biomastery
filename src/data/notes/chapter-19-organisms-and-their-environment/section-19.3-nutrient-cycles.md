## :supplement[**Nitrogen cycle**]

- :supplement[One of the products of the decay of animal and plant protein is ammonia - ammonia dissolves readily in water to form ammonium ions which is washed into the soil]
- :supplement[The excretory products of animals also contain nitrogenous waste products like ammonia, urea, and uric acid -  urea that is formed in the liver is a result of deamination ( removal of the nitrogen containing part of amino acids)]

### :supplement[**Processes that add nitrates to the soil**]

#### :supplement[**Nitrification (by nitrifying bacteria):**]

### :supplement[**They live in the soil and use ammonia from decaying organic matter as a source of energy - during this process of nitrification, the bacteria produce nitrates**]

- :supplement[They oxidise ammonia to ammonium compounds to nitrites to nitrates]

:supplement[Plants can take up ammonia from the soil but they take up nitrates more easily - so nitrifying bacteria increase the fertility of the soil by increasing nitrate availability to plants]

#### :supplement[**Nitrogen fixing bacteria:**]

:supplement[Absorb nitrogen as a gas from the air spaces in the soil and build it into compounds of ammonia]

#### :supplement[**Lightning:**]

:supplement[The high temperature of lightning discharge causes some of the nitrogen and oxygen in the air to combine and form oxides of nitrogen - these dissolve in the rain and are washed into the soil as weak acids where they form nitrates.]

### :supplement[**Processes that remove nitrates from the soil**]

#### :supplement[**Uptake by plants:**]

:supplement[Plant roots absorb nitrates from the soil and combine them with the carbohydrates to make amino acids which are built into proteins]

#### :supplement[**Denitrifying bacteria:**]

:supplement[They obtain their energy by breaking down nitrates to nitrogen gas which then escape from the soil into the atmosphere]
