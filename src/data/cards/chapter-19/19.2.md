Describe food chains:

<!-- answer -->

Describe a food web:

<!-- answer -->

a network of interconnected food chains
<!-- card -->

Describe a producer:

<!-- answer -->

an organism that makes its own organic nutrients, usually using energy from sunlight,
though photosynthesis
<!-- card -->

Describe a consumer:

<!-- answer -->

an organism that gets its energy by feeding on other organisms
<!-- card -->

Describe herbivore:

<!-- answer -->

an animal that gets its energy by eating plants
<!-- card -->

Describe carnivore:

<!-- answer -->

an animal that gets its energy by eating other animals
<!-- card -->

Describe a decomposer:

<!-- answer -->

an organism that gets its energy from dead organic material

biomass to represent a food chain?

<!-- answer -->

- shows energy transfer
- always upright
- not affected by organism size
- most accurate representation
- accounts for energy loss

<!-- card -->

Why is the transfer of energy from one trophic level to another often not efficient?

<!-- answer -->

- not all of the organism is eaten
- energy is lost as heat during respiration
- energy is lost during movement
- energy is lost in urine/faeces
- only 10% of energy is transferred to the next trophic level

<!-- card -->

Why do food chains usually have fewer than five trophic levels (5 marks)?

<!-- answer -->

- energy is lost between trophic levels
- only 10% of energy is transferred at each trophic level
- energy is lost in respiration/heat/movement/(named) metabolic process
- energy is lost as not all of the organism is eaten
- there is not enough energy to support a 5th trophic level
